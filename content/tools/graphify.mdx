Graphify-Labs' Graphify, which R21 adapted for Windows. It turns a codebase and
its docs, schemas and configs into a queryable knowledge graph using local
deterministic AST parsing — every edge explained, no vector store, no embedding
step.

## What deterministic parsing buys

The usual way to make a codebase searchable by an assistant is to embed it and
retrieve by similarity. That works until you need an answer that is structural
rather than topical: what calls this, what breaks if it changes, what is the path
from the route to the query. Similarity search answers those with things that
*read* like the answer.

An AST-derived graph answers them with edges. It is also reproducible — the same
repository produces the same graph, so a wrong answer is a bug you can find rather
than a retrieval that happened to rank badly today.

## Credit, stated precisely

The upstream tool is Graphify-Labs' work. R21 maintains a Windows adaptation, and
the skill in our workspace declares itself `graphify-windows` for that reason. Both
halves of that sentence matter, and neither is an achievement worth blurring into
the other.

## Two attributions that were wrong internally

This entry exists partly to correct R21's own record, because the site's whole
premise is that claims survive being checked — including ours.

- **It is not by "Chase AI."** That is the person whose video covered the tool.
  A section heading in R21's internal wiki turned coverage into authorship, and it
  was repeated from there more than once. Sourcing a project's author from a video
  about the project is how this happens.
- **A "~60K stars" figure circulated internally.** It was accurate to the date of
  that recording and to nothing since. Star counts here are fetched at verification
  time or omitted; they are never carried across from a note.

Neither error was caught by re-reading the note. Both were caught by querying the
registry, which is why the build now requires it.
